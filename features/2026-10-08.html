Bargaining with the Data Center

Knowledge Problem · Oct 07

Data center developments are increasingly subject to Community Benefits Agreements (CBAs), which function as Coasean bargains that allocate scarce environmental and regulatory resources between developers and host communities. These agreements combine externality mitigation (e.g., noise, water use limits) with surplus-sharing mechanisms (e.g., community funds), but their economic efficiency depends on enforceability, timing, and nexus to actual project impacts—key risks include rent extraction and holdup, especially when demands arise after irreversible investments. Notable examples: Lancaster, PA’s draft CBA includes $20M in funds and strict operational caps; Cedar Rapids’ deals with QTS ($18M fund) and Google ($36M fund) highlight the need to assess net benefits after accounting for tax incentives.
