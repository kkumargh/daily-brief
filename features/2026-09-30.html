The Energy Transition’s Two Speeds

Off the Charts · Sep 30

The energy transition is bifurcated: renewables are rapidly displacing fossil fuels in power generation—now 33% of global electricity, led by Europe and China—but fossil fuels remain entrenched across transport, industry, and heating, with global oil, coal, and gas consumption near record highs. Despite $300B+ annual clean energy investment in China and growing solar capacity (1.2 TW), OECD declines in emissions are outweighed by rising demand in Asia, keeping global CO2 emissions above 40B tonnes; meanwhile, U.S. data center demand (projected +97 GW by 2028) risks a 57 GW power gap, driving $50B in fossil fuel power investment by 2026. Actionable view: Overweight renewable infrastructure (e.g., TAN) but expect sustained capex in gas generation (XOM, COP) due to AI-driven power demand and grid constraints.
