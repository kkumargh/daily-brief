Alpha in Academia · Jul 05

Adverse Selection Eats the Spread: Market makers in CME futures capture a small spread but lose almost the same amount to adverse selection, resulting in near-zero profit. Under market stress, losses exceed the spread, making passive liquidity provision a break-even or losing proposition. (CME futures)

Disagreement and Returns: Disagreement about company fundamentals predicts lower future returns (0.7% per month), while disagreement over noise has no predictive power. Crude aggregate measures of investor disagreement can mislead, as they often track sentiment rather than true disagreement. (No specific ticker)

Bond ETF Fragility: Bond ETFs dislocate from their NAV during market stress due to the costly redemption process, not investor redemptions. The deepest discounts occur in safer, more liquid segments, highlighting the structural risk in ETFs. (Bond ETFs, e.g., MUB, AGG)

Enduring Influence of Openness: Cities opened to foreign trade in 19th-century China continue to exhibit higher risk-taking behavior, as evidenced by historical and modern investment patterns. This suggests regional risk appetite is culturally inherited. (No specific ticker)
