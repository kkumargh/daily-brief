Cieslak, Morse, and Vissing-Jørgensen (CMVJ) originally found that from 1994–2013, nearly all US equity risk premia accrued during "even" weeks of the FOMC cycle—days 0–3 and 4–8 relative to meeting dates—suggesting informed trading ahead of Fed information leaks. Extending the analysis beyond 2013, however, shows the effect disappears in out-of-sample periods, indicating the anomaly did not persist and likely reflected a now-defunct market inefficiency. The Rise and Fall of the FOMC Cycle

Alpha in Academia · Sep 25

The core thesis of Cieslak, Morse, and Vissing-Jørgensen (CMVJ) posits that from 1994 to 2013, nearly all the U.S. equity risk premium was earned during "even" weeks of the FOMC cycle—defined by a mechanical calendar around Fed announcement dates—with average daily excess returns of ~12 bps in even weeks versus near-zero in odd weeks, suggesting informed trading ahead of Fed information leaks. However, out-of-sample data through 2026 shows the pattern collapses, with no sustained edge post-2013, invalidating the strategy’s forward applicability and underscoring its likely spurious or regime-dependent nature. No actionable market view or trade survives; the result is a cautionary tale on overfitting and the decay of empirical anomalies.
