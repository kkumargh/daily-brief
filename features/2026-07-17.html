Nonbank Subsidiaries and the Hidden Fragility of Internal Capital Markets Reallocation

libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org · Jul 17

Basel III regulations led banks to become safer by reallocating capital from nonbank subsidiaries to bank subsidiaries, but this reallocation has left nonbank affiliates with thinner capital buffers and riskier business models, potentially increasing the overall fragility of bank holding companies (BHCs). Stress tests show that under a 5% loss on nonbank assets, the average BHC would need to deploy 18% of its excess capital to support these affiliates, with some BHCs facing the risk of exhausting their capital buffers entirely. This suggests that while banks appear safer, the risk has merely shifted to less visible parts of the organization, posing a hidden threat to financial stability. Ticker: BKX (KBW Bank Index)
