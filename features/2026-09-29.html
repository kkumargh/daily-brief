Most Investors Get Margin of Safety Wrong

Schwar Capital · Sep 28

Schwar Capital argues that the widely misunderstood "margin of safety" in investing—popularly defined as buying below intrinsic value—is incomplete and misleading when based on uncertain forecasts. True margin of safety, per Graham’s original framework, is an accruing *rate* differential between a business’s earnings yield and a safe alternative (e.g., bonds), not a static discount to a modeled value, and it must persist even if key growth assumptions prove wrong. Actionable takeaway: Focus on businesses with durable earning power that trade at yields significantly above risk-free rates, and assess margin of safety under stressed assumptions—not base-case optimism.
