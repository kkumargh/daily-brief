What the Managers Are Saying

November 22, 2026 · 22 letters, summarized

Dominant Themes

  • Skepticism toward AI-driven capex sustainability and valuations: Multiple managers express concern over the financial viability of AI infrastructure spending, citing stretched valuations, overinvestment risks, and reliance on external financing. This includes [5] (exiting "neo-cloud" AI firms), [9] (caution on AI financing sustainability), [14] (AI valuations under pressure), [15] (bullish but concentrated), [16] (constructive on chipmakers despite concerns), [19] (exiting INTC on weak outlook), [20] (exiting semiconductor capex-sensitive names), and [21] (bearish on dominant AI tech, citing $900B compute commitments from Anthropic).
  • Rotation into value, real assets, and income-oriented equities: A broad shift away from momentum/AI stocks toward undervalued, cash-generative, or dividend-paying businesses is evident across styles and regions. Seen in [1] (6% Real Assets, 39% Compounders), [2] (overweight Reversionary/Real Assets), [7] (added insurers, food producers), [8] (increased value exposure), [9] (focus on valuation discipline), [12] (undervalued European small/mid-caps), [17] (franked dividend focus), and [22] (energy, defensive sectors).
  • Macro caution amid late-cycle risks and fiscal instability: Managers widely cite slowing growth, sticky inflation, and rising unemployment as key headwinds. Fiscal concerns—especially in France—are highlighted by [11] (fiscal instability), [13] (exited French debt), [14] (French sovereign risks), and [22] (cautious on cyclical demand). The “age of shocks” framing in [11] and [13] reflects structural uncertainty.
  • Active positioning in government bonds as safe-haven assets: Several fixed-income managers are overweight high-quality sovereigns, betting on risk-off flows and falling yields. [13] is explicitly bullish on Gilts and JGBs, [11] favors UK/AU duration, and [5] sees lower global yields from de-escalation. This contrasts with equity-focused caution.
  • Opportunistic optimism in spinoffs, reindustrialization, and governance-driven value: Activist and bottom-up strategies are targeting catalysts like spinoffs ([2]), governance reform in Korea ([3]), and structural reindustrialization ([2], [14]). [3] is actively engaged in Korean AGMs, while [2] cites "humility phase" sectors and reindustrialization beneficiaries.

Cross-Currents & Disagreements

  • AI investment outlook: [15] and [16] remain bullish on hyperscaler capex and AI infrastructure (Meta, Microsoft, Naspers/Prosus via Tencent), viewing it as foundational for growth. In contrast, [5], [9], [19], [20], and [21] express clear skepticism, citing overinvestment, funding unsustainability, and valuation bubbles—[21] even points to $900B in future compute commitments as a red flag.
  • Japanese assets: [13] is aggressively long JGBs and JPY, citing structural repatriation and carry improvement. However, [20] exits Japanese semiconductor equipment makers (Tokyo Electron, Renesas) due to capex cyclicality fears, reflecting a more cautious view on Japan’s tech exposure despite structural resilience elsewhere.
  • French fiscal risk response: [11], [12], and [13] all highlight France as a concern, with [13] exiting French government debt entirely. Yet [22] maintains a 24% energy overweight in its International Portfolio—likely including French energy names—and shows no indication of avoiding French exposure, suggesting divergent risk assessments.

Non-Consensus / Contrarian Flag

The most contrarian call is Invenomic Fund (BIVIX)’s sustained bearishness on dominant AI tech names ([21]), reinforced by their observation of $900B in future compute commitments from Anthropic’s S-1—framed not as progress but as evidence of an unsustainable capital burn. While others are cautious, Invenomic is actively short (net +30% long but short book contributed +10.39% in October) and sees a *prolonged, volatile normalization* ahead, expecting Technology sector valuation (still at 4.7x S&P 500) to mean-revert from dotcom-era extremes. This is the only manager explicitly framing AI as a structural bubble with quantifiable overhang, while most peers are either selectively cautious or still invested.
