What the Managers Are Saying

November 8, 2026 · 27 letters, summarized

Dominant Themes

  • AI skepticism amid capex concerns: Multiple managers express caution around the sustainability of AI-driven capital spending, citing negative free cash flow, speculative IPO failures (e.g., Firmus), and ROI scrutiny (see [10], [11], [12], [13], [26]). Some still back AI enablers but avoid overhyped names (e.g., [25] avoids GOOGL/META/INTC; [26] shifts from semis to software).
  • Defensive positioning in small- and mid-caps: A consistent tilt toward quality, cash-generative businesses with pricing power and strong balance sheets—especially in Sweden and Australia—amid tighter monetary policy and macro uncertainty (see [2], [3], [4], [10], [11], [12], [13], [23]).
  • Swedish small-cap outperformance via stock-picking: Several funds (TIN, Spiltan variants, Protean) highlight strong returns in October driven by concentrated holdings in Swedish healthcare, industrials, and software (e.g., Addnode, Sectra, Vimian), outperforming local benchmarks by wide margins (see [1], [2], [3], [4], [17]).
  • "Higher for longer" rates as structural, not cyclical: Widespread acceptance that elevated rates are here to stay due to fiscal deficits, central bank hawkishness, and inflation persistence—driving underweights in rate-sensitive sectors like real estate and long-duration bonds (see [8], [10], [12], [19], [27]).
  • Active vs. passive decay narrative: Growing concern that passive flows, ETF dominance (~52% market share), and algorithmic trading are eroding fundamental analysis, creating opportunities for active value investors (see [17], [25]).

Cross-Currents & Disagreements

  • AI capex: structural boom vs. speculative froth

*Bullish*: [9] sees AI as a multi-year tailwind accelerating growth at data platforms (RELX, LSEG); [21] doubles down on SandboxAQ’s commercialization and government backing.

*Bearish*: [10], [12], [13] warn of unsustainable FCF and IPO collapse (Firmus); [26] acknowledges deceleration to 15–20% growth and rotation out of semis.

  • U.S. tech valuations: buy the dip vs. avoid rich multiples

*Constructive*: [24] exited META/NFLX at highs but remains optimistic on U.S. equities long-term; [20] sees rerating potential in "neglected" compounders.

*Cautious*: [8] underweights U.S. equities entirely; [27] remains underweight due to rich valuations and energy cost risks.

  • Vietnam outlook: deep value vs. liquidity crisis

*Constructive*: [7] highlights 9.95% GDP growth, FDI surge, and structural shift into tech manufacturing (Samsung).

*Cautious*: [6] acknowledges attractive valuations but flags record-low foreign ownership (12.9%) and $4.6bn YTD outflows as systemic constraints.

Non-Consensus / Contrarian Flag

The most contrarian call is Cedar Creek Partners’ new position in Rivian (RIVN) at current levels as an “asymmetric bet” on EV market share and margin improvement ([24]). While nearly every other manager avoids or exits unprofitable EV names (e.g., Pender lost on LCID/RIVN, [18]), and macro sentiment on EVs is weak, Cedar Creek sees value where others see risk—amid widespread EV sector pessimism and Rivian’s deep valuation discount. This stands in stark contrast to the broader flight from capital-intensive, low-FCF growth stories.
