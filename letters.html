What the Managers Are Saying

August 8, 2026 · 65 letters, summarized

Dominant Themes

  • AI Infrastructure Skepticism & Selective Engagement: Multiple managers express caution around AI-related valuations, avoiding broad exposure while selectively investing in beneficiaries (e.g., power, semiconductors) or companies with durable moats. (see [1], [2], [4], [16], [30], [34], [51], [62])
  • Small-Cap Valuation Gap and Reversal Thesis: A widespread belief that small caps are deeply undervalued relative to large caps, with several managers positioning for a potential multi-year outperformance cycle driven by earnings acceleration and multiple expansion. (see [1], [5], [11], [12], [19], [31], [62])
  • Geopolitical Risk as Catalyst, Not Just Risk: The Iran conflict and broader Middle East tensions are viewed not only as sources of volatility but as catalysts for energy inflation, supply chain shifts, and investment opportunities in frontier markets and defense. (see [1], [22], [23], [24], [48], [55])
  • Value Rotation Amid Overvalued Growth: A clear consensus that large-cap tech and AI momentum stocks are overextended, prompting rotation into undervalued, cash-generative businesses—especially in financials, industrials, and niche value plays. (see [4], [14], [16], [17], [30], [55], [59])
  • Active Capital Allocation and Shareholder Engagement: Emphasis on activist involvement, spin-offs, and capital discipline as key value drivers, with managers citing direct engagement (e.g., letters, board pressure) to unlock value. (see [6], [39], [40], [49], [61])

Cross-Currents & Disagreements

  • AI Disruption: Threat vs. Non-Event: Some managers (e.g., [51], [64]) warn of AI as a structural threat to software and services, while others (e.g., [3], [26], [49]) dismiss disruption fears as overblown, citing historical resilience and long-term cash flow durability. (see [3] vs. [51])
  • Cash Positioning: Offense vs. Defense: While some funds (e.g., [45], [46]) hold >75% cash due to overvaluation concerns, others (e.g., [24], [48]) are deploying capital aggressively into war-affected or AI-beneficiary plays. (see [45] vs. [48])
  • Semiconductor Exposure: Buy vs. Sell: Managers diverge sharply on chip stocks—some adding (e.g., [16] on LITE, COHU), while others exit due to supply risks (e.g., [61] warns of Chinese memory flooding) or valuation concerns (e.g., [64] trimmed AMD). (see [16] vs. [61])

Non-Consensus / Contrarian Flag

The most contrarian call is Praetorian Capital’s "Economic Feudalism" thesis, betting on inflation beneficiaries, long volatility, and exchange volumes amid the Hormuz conflict, with a massive 38% gain in Marex (MRX) already realized and a $120 price target implying further upside. This macro-driven, volatility-long stance stands in stark contrast to the broader cohort’s focus on fundamental value or AI skepticism. (see [48])
