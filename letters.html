What the Managers Are Saying

November 7, 2026 · 21 letters, summarized

Dominant Themes

  • Cautious stance on AI-driven capex and speculative tech: Multiple funds express skepticism about the sustainability of AI-related capital spending, citing negative free cash flow, frothy valuations, and recent IPO withdrawals (e.g., Nvidia-backed Firmus). This is evident across Spheria funds ([4], [5], [6], [8]), CDT Capital ([19]), and BlueBox ([20]). Concerns extend to overhyped SI/AI names like GOOGL, META, INTC ([19]), and unprofitable AI plays ([2]).
  • Defensive positioning amid “higher-for-longer” rates: A consensus view on persistent monetary tightening appears in [1], [4], [6], [7], [13], and [21], with 10-year yields at 5.25% and RBA rates at 4.85% (highest since 2008). Funds are emphasizing balance sheet strength, free cash flow, and resilience—seen in allocations to cash (up to 7% in [6], 14.9% in [16]), Treasuries ([2]), and quality industrials/consumer staples ([3], [17]).
  • Rotation away from large-cap tech dominance toward overlooked quality: Several managers highlight the distortion from passive flows and mega-cap concentration, favoring active stock-picking in undervalued, cash-generative businesses outside index weightings ([2], [9], [14], [16], [19]). Examples include GNTX, AVA, SFD ([2]), CRH, TTEK ([16]), and non-bank lenders WZR, PLT ([17]).
  • UK digital champions as AI beneficiaries, not losers: Contrary to narrative, [3] and [14] show strong rebound in RELX, LSEG, Experian, and Sage on real AI adoption (e.g., 95% AI uptake at LSEG, Legal division growth doubling at RELX), positioning UK data/software firms as structural winners—supported by improving trading updates and cross-selling dynamics.

Cross-Currents & Disagreements

  • AI infrastructure: foundational long vs. speculative avoid – [19] maintains long positions in semiconductor design, power, and hyperscaler data centers as core AI enablers, while explicitly avoiding overhyped names like GOOGL, META, INTC. In contrast, [8] exited *Firmus*, an AI-driven data infrastructure microcap, pre-withdrawal, citing speculative risk—indicating divergent risk tolerance within the same theme.
  • U.S. tech valuations: avoid vs. accumulate – [21] is underweight U.S. tech due to rich multiples and energy cost pressures, while [9] sees attractive large-cap valuations (Top 100 at 29x P/E vs. 10-year avg of 30x) and advocates accumulating high-conviction names. Similarly, [18] exited META and NFLX on valuation but initiated RIVN—showing internal rotation rather than full sector exit.

Non-Consensus / Contrarian Flag

  • SandboxAQ (private) as core strategic holding ([15]) – While most funds are trimming or avoiding speculative AI names, Horizon Kinetics is doubling down on a pre-IPO, physics-based AI play with commercial traction via AWS, Google Cloud, and Anthropic. With a $500M CHIPS R&D award, pharma partnerships (AstraZeneca, J&J), and open-source agent collaboration tools, it’s betting on *decentralized, non-LVM-centric AI infrastructure*—a sharp contrast to the dominant large-language model narrative. This is the most differentiated thesis in the set.
