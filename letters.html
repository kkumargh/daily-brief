What the Managers Are Saying

November 15, 2026 · 68 letters, summarized

Dominant Themes

  • Defensive rotation into cash, value, and resilient cash flows amid macro uncertainty: multiple funds (Ruffer [7], [8], [9], [16], [23], [24], [25]; Liontrust [33], [35], [36], [37], [39], [41], [42], [43], [46], [47]; Malborough [3], [4]) are reducing exposure to high-growth/AI tech, increasing cash or bond duration, and rotating into utilities, banks, healthcare, and high free cash flow equities.
  • Skepticism toward AI capex sustainability and overvalued "neocloud" trades: Ruffer [7], [8], [9], [16], [23], [24], [25], Malborough [3], [5], WCM [13], Liontrust [40], Harding Loevner [56], [57], and Pender [28], [29] express caution on AI-driven valuations, citing weak free cash flow, monetization risks, and narrowing leadership.
  • Japan as a structural reform story with governance-driven value unlocking: AVI [65], [66], [67], [68], Nakano [1], Lindsell Train [48], and TAMIM [18] highlight shareholder return improvements, TOB events (e.g., Broadmedia, Sharingtechnology), and JPX governance reforms as catalysts for small-cap outperformance.
  • Geopolitical and energy-driven inflation persistence: Malborough [3], Ruffer [7], [8], [9], [16], [23], [24], [25], Liontrust [43], [45], [47], and Lowell [32] cite Brent crude >$117, Gulf escalation risks, and physical commodity shortages (copper, uranium, LNG) as inflationary tailwinds, supporting energy and infrastructure.
  • Small-cap underperformance and risk-off sentiment: Wilson [11], Sandon [21], Regal [26], Glennon [58], and Salter [22] note sharp underperformance in small caps due to funding cost pressures, AI derating, and macro sensitivity, with some (e.g., Wilson, Liontrust) seeing opportunity in dislocated quality names.

Cross-Currents & Disagreements

  • AI infrastructure exposure: Ruffer, Malborough, and Harding Loevner are broadly exiting or underweighting AI hardware (SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron) due to capex and FCF concerns ([3], [53], [56], [57]), while Bell Global [63] and Bailador [64] are adding to ASML and private AI enablers, arguing for sustained demand.
  • Japan equity positioning: Lindsell Train [48] is underweight AI infrastructure but overweight quality compounders like Nintendo and OBIC, while Nakano [1] and AVI [65], [66] are overweight small-cap industrials and governance plays—divergent paths within the same market.
  • Brazilian risk post-election: Somma [20] is de-risking Brazilian equities post-Lula, while Drys Capital [60] is increasing exposure to Petrobras and utilities, betting on post-correction value and sector resilience.

Non-Consensus / Contrarian Flag

  • Fat Prophets Global Contrarian Fund [59] is going long Amazon (AMZN) and Japanese financials (SMFG, MFG) amid a broad tech sell-off and rising rates, a stark contrarian bet when most managers (Ruffer, Malborough, Liontrust) are reducing AI/tech exposure. This positions them against the dominant defensive rotation, betting on earnings resilience and re-rating potential in oversold leaders.
