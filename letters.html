What the Managers Are Saying

November 7, 2026 · 162 letters, summarized

Dominant Themes

  • AI Infrastructure Skepticism & Value Rotation: A growing number of managers are actively rotating out of or avoiding overvalued AI infrastructure and semiconductor names (e.g., NVDA, AMD, AI data centers), citing unsustainable capex, negative free cash flow, and overhyped valuations. This is a core theme for [25], [34], [35], [138], [140], [161], and [153], who are instead pivoting toward undervalued, cash-generative businesses in industrials, financials, and real assets (see [1], [6], [62], [65]).
  • Defensive Positioning in a "Higher-for-Longer" Rate Environment: Multiple managers highlight elevated bond yields (10Y UST at ~5.3%) and persistent inflation as key macro headwinds, leading to a defensive stance. They are favoring companies with strong balance sheets, durable cash flows, and pricing power to withstand higher capital costs (see [34], [35], [8], [9], [147], [158]).
  • Geopolitical Risk as a Persistent Macro Driver: The conflict in Iran and its impact on oil prices (Brent ~$100) is a near-universal concern, creating a "wildcard" for inflation and economic growth (see [1], [5], [17], [51], [52], [59], [143]). This has led to active risk management, including exits from vulnerable EMs (Egypt, Turkey, UAE) and increased allocations to energy, commodities, and defensive sectors (see [51], [52], [117]).
  • Active Rotation into Undervalued Real Assets & "Boring" Industrials: A significant cohort is moving capital into tangible, inflation-resistant assets like land, water, energy infrastructure, and undervalued industrial businesses, viewing them as beneficiaries of the AI buildout rather than direct participants (see [62], [65], [99], [100], [147]). This reflects a belief in "economic feudalism" where essential resources command premium returns.
  • Tactical Shift to UK, Japan, and EM Equities, Underweighting US Tech: A clear consensus among Liontrust funds ([73]-[80], [86]-[93]) and others ([74], [75], [77], [117]) favors markets with more attractive valuations, such as the UK (driven by energy, financials, and governance reforms), Japan (corporate governance), and select EMs, while maintaining a significant underweight to the US due to stretched valuations.

Cross-Currents & Disagreements

  • AI Infrastructure Valuation: [18], [19], and [153] are aggressively investing in AI infrastructure (e.g., Enova, Rackspace) and related capex plays, expecting massive productivity gains. In direct contrast, [161] and [138] have exited these same names (NVDA, SMH, AMD) due to concerns over negative free cash flow and overvaluation, calling it a speculative bubble.
  • Alphabet (GOOGL) Positioning: [34], [35], and [150] are adding to Alphabet, citing disciplined AI investment and dividend growth, viewing it as a quality AI play. Conversely, [54] and [103] have reduced or exited their positions due to concerns over regulatory scrutiny and valuation.
  • Tesla (TSLA) Sentiment: [33] and [56] are adding to Tesla, recognizing its leadership in EVs and energy. In stark disagreement, [32], [103], [104], and [105] have exited or sold their positions due to valuation concerns, increasing competition, and overhyped expectations.

Non-Consensus / Contrarian Flag

The most contrarian call is Horizon Kinetics' Bitcoin Cash Portfolio ([64]), which maintains a 100% allocation to Bitcoin Cash, a highly controversial and low-market-cap cryptocurrency. The manager projects a fair value of $150,000–$270,000 by 2028 based on power-law dynamics, a view diametrically opposed to the mainstream financial world and even to other crypto-focused funds. This is a pure, high-conviction bet on a single, highly volatile asset, making it the most differentiated position in the set.
