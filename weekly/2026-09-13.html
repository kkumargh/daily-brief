Macro Overview — Week ending 2026-09-13

The Week in Macro

  • Markets moved from expecting the Fed to hold to expecting a hike. Odds of a 2026 rate hike rose from 71.5% to 86.5%. The 2-year Treasury yield, which tracks expected Fed policy most closely, jumped 22bp to 4.56%. Both measures point the same way: markets now see the Fed tightening, not easing.
  • Long-term yields are close to 5%. The 10-year rose 18bp to 4.95%, and the odds it hits 5.0% before 2027 went from 29.5% to 86.5%, the biggest move of the week. Because short yields rose more than long ones, the 10Y–2Y spread narrowed from 0.41 to 0.33. That pattern usually means the market is pricing a tighter Fed, not stronger long-run growth.
  • Growth expectations went up, not down. The odds of 2026 GDP growth landing at 1.5–2.0% fell from 44.85% to 16.2%, while the 2.0–2.5% range rose from 46.5% to 54.5%. The data mostly supports this: August payrolls rose 162k, unemployment held at 4.1%, and July industrial production and job openings both edged up. The weak spots are July housing starts (down 12.4%) and retail sales (down 0.6%).
  • Inflation pressure is building. WTI crude rose 5.6% to $97.26, and August core CPI rose 0.29% month over month. Higher oil feeds into inflation expectations, which helps explain why hike odds rose even though inflation data wasn't alarming.
  • Risk appetite is holding up so far. The Chicago Fed's financial conditions index barely moved (–0.560 to –0.564) and conditions remain loose. The broad dollar slipped 0.57%, but that reading runs only to Sept 4, so it doesn't yet reflect this week's rise in yields.
