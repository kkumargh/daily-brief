The Week in Macro

  • Fewer expected Fed hikes, but higher long-term yields. Prediction markets cut the odds of the Fed's upper bound reaching 4.5% before 2027 from 46% to 16%. The upper bound sits at 4.0% today. Two-year Treasury yields agreed, falling 9bp to 4.78%. Ten-year yields rose 6bp to 5.24% anyway. That gap widened the 10Y–2Y spread to 0.45% (+9bp) and the 10Y–3M spread to 1.09% (+16bp). So the long end isn't rising on fears of a tighter Fed. It looks like investors are demanding more to hold long-dated debt.
  • Prediction markets raised their growth view while the labor data softened. Odds of 2026 GDP growth above 2.5% jumped from 27% to 46%. Retail sales support that, up 1.1% in August. The labor data point the other way: payrolls rose only 29k, unemployment edged up to 4.2%, job openings fell by 256k to 7.08M, and Michigan sentiment is at 51.7. Spending is holding up, but hiring is slowing.

  • Risk appetite held up. The trade-weighted dollar rose 0.7% to 120.33, and the Chicago Fed's financial conditions index was basically unchanged at -0.548, which is still loose. Nothing this week suggests stress.
