The Week in Macro

  • The curve flattened from the front end — the week's cleanest signal. The 10Y–3M spread narrowed from +0.92 to +0.78 (−14bp), while 10Y–2Y barely moved (+0.47→+0.46). Long yields sat still (10Y 4.68→4.69, 2Y 4.23→4.25); the flattening came from the bill end, meaning markets pulled *back* on near-term Fed cuts even as the 2s10s slope held. Policy is on hold — the effective fed funds rate was unchanged at 3.63%.
  • Labor data is sending mixed signals, and the softness is under the surface. Unemployment ticked *down* to 4.1% from 4.2%, but payrolls actually shrank slightly (158,881k→158,858k, −23k), job openings fell 178k to 7,359k, and continuing claims rose 24k to 1.801mn. A lower jobless rate alongside falling payrolls and rising continuing claims usually means people are leaving the labor force, not finding work. Hiring is cooling faster than firing is rising.
  • Inflation gave the Fed room, quietly. Core CPI edged *down* (336.121→336.065, −0.02%) and core PCE rose just 0.13% on the month — an annualized pace comfortably in the low-1s. Combined with WTI falling 2.7% ($84.25→$81.96), the inflation impulse weakened this week. That contrasts with the front-end flattening: the bond market is not yet trading this as a green light to cut.
  • The dollar softened and financial conditions stayed easy. The broad trade-weighted dollar fell 0.83% (120.71→119.70) while the Chicago Fed NFCI was essentially flat at −0.529 — still meaningfully loose. Easy conditions plus a weaker dollar are a risk-supportive backdrop, and cheaper oil is a real-income tailwind for consumers.
  • The consumer and housing are the upside surprise. Housing starts jumped 19.0% (1,199k→1,427k) and Michigan sentiment rose 10.5% (44.8→49.5) — though both are monthly series off very depressed bases, so treat the level, not the percentage, as the story: sentiment at 49.5 is still historically weak. Retail sales grew only 0.22%.
