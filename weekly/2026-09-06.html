Macro Overview — Week ending 2026-09-06

The Week in Macro

  • The bond market repriced higher, and prediction markets sprinted to catch up. The 10-year yield rose 10bp on the week to 4.77% (from 4.67%) and the 2-year jumped 14bp to 4.34%. Traders on Polymarket now put a 92.9% chance the 10-year touches 4.8% before 2027, up from 67.5% a week ago — a 25-point leap that is really just the market acknowledging we are 3bp away. The more informative move is the 5.0% market, at 29.5% from 17.5%.
  • This is a supply/inflation-premium selloff, not a growth scare. The 10Y-2Y spread widened to 0.41 from 0.39 and 10Y-3M to 0.87 from 0.83 — long rates led. Financial conditions (Chicago Fed NFCI at -0.558 vs -0.556) barely budged and remain accommodative, so nothing here looks like stress.
  • Oil did the heavy lifting on the inflation side. WTI rose 9.0% in a week, from $83.90 to $91.48. That single move explains most of the term-premium story: a near-$8 jump in crude feeds directly into headline inflation and into the "higher-for-longer" case that lifted both yield markets.
  • The underlying inflation data has not confirmed it — yet. Core CPI rose 0.22% and core PCE 0.25% on the latest monthly prints, both consistent with roughly 2.5-3% annualized. The gap between benign core data and a hawkish rates repricing is the week's central tension.
  • The consumer is a split screen. Michigan sentiment surged 11.5% (49.5→55.2) and job openings rose to 7.271mm from 7.182mm, but retail sales *fell* 0.58% and housing starts collapsed 12.4% to 1.239mm from 1.415mm. Households feel better and are spending less — and 4.77% mortgage-adjacent yields are visibly biting housing.
