Macro Overview — Week ending 2026-07-26

The Week in Macro

  • The week's dominant story was rates repricing higher, and markets and data agreed. The 2Y yield rose 21bp (4.16%→4.37%) and the 10Y 14bp (4.57%→4.71%), while Polymarket's odds of a Fed rate *hike* in 2026 jumped from 54.5% to 71.5%. The FRED data corroborates the market: this is a genuine hawkish repricing, not a positioning wobble.
  • Growth data refuses to break. Unemployment fell to 4.2% from 4.3%, housing starts surged 19% (1,199k→1,427k), payrolls added 57k and continuing claims edged down 2k. That resilience is exactly what takes cuts off the table — and it explains why the odds of the 10Y dipping below 3.9% collapsed from 26.7% to 10.1%.
  • Oil is doing the inflation work. WTI rose 6.5% on the week (79.20→84.38). With core PCE up 0.32% m/m and core CPI roughly flat (-0.02%), the headline inflation impulse is now coming from energy, not the underlying trend — but it's enough to keep the hawkish repricing honest.
  • The one dissonant note is the consumer. Michigan sentiment fell 10% (49.8→44.8) and retail sales grew just 0.22%. Households are unhappy while the labor data looks fine — a gap worth watching, and the most likely source of a growth scare that would reverse this week's rate move.
  • Risk appetite has not cracked. The Chicago Fed financial conditions index eased slightly further (-0.539→-0.552, i.e. looser), the 10Y-3M spread steepened to +0.73, and the broad dollar was flat (+0.02%). Higher yields are being read as strength, not stress.
